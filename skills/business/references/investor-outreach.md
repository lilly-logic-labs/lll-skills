# Investor outreach

Find the right investors, write short honest messages, follow up without nagging, and prepare for the meeting. Drafts only: the owner sends every message. No invented investors, portfolio companies, quotes or warm intros. Outreach to prospective customers is `lll:marketing`.

## 1. Fit before volume

Build a list of 30 to 60 investors, not 500. For each, record only public facts, each with a source link:

| Field | Source |
|---|---|
| Name, firm, role | Firm site, professional profile |
| Stage and check size they state | Firm site, their own posts |
| Sectors and geographies | Firm site, portfolio |
| Recent investments in the last 12 months (names, dates) | Portfolio page, press, filings |
| Conflicts (they back a direct competitor) | Portfolio |
| Route in | Mutual contact, past founder, event, content they wrote |
| Notes on how they work | Their posts, podcasts, founder reviews |

Score each 0 to 3 on stage fit, sector fit, check-size fit, recent activity and route in. Contact the 5 to 10 highest first. Ignore anyone whose stated stage or check size does not match; a polite no from a bad fit teaches nothing.

## 2. Routes, best first

1. Warm introduction from someone who knows both sides and can say something specific.
2. A founder in the investor's portfolio who will make a short introduction.
3. Events, demo days, accelerator and community pitch sessions.
4. Investor-run programmes and public application forms.
5. A short, relevant cold message to an investor who accepts them.

Ask for introductions in a form that is easy to forward (see the forwardable blurb below). Do not ask someone to introduce you to people you have not researched.

## 3. Cold message (under 150 words)

```
Subject: <Company>: <one-line description> (<stage>, <traction in a phrase>)

Hi <first name>,

<One sentence on why them: a specific investment or post, with the detail>.

We are building <what>, for <whom>. <One proof point with number and date>.

We are raising <amount> to reach <milestone> by <date>. 

Would a 20-minute call in the next two weeks make sense? I can send a one-pager and the deck first.

<Name>, <role>, <link to site or demo>
```

Rules: one proof point, not five. Real numbers only. No attachments on first contact unless asked (a link is fine). No flattery that could apply to anyone. Plain text, no tracking pixels.

## 4. Forwardable blurb (for introductions)

```
<Name> is building <what> for <whom>. In <time>, they <proof>. They are raising <amount> and I thought of you because <specific reason>. If it is not a fit, no problem; I will not be offended.
```

Write it so the introducer needs no edits.

## 5. Follow-up cadence

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 5 to 7 days after no reply | One short bump: a new piece of news (a customer, a number, a launch) |
| 10 to 14 days later | A last note with a one-line update and an easy "no" |
| Then | Stop. Add them to an update list only if they said yes to updates |
| After a meeting | Thank-you within 24 hours; the promised material; a 3-line summary of next steps |
| While they consider | A short monthly update with real numbers (what grew, what broke, what you learned, what you need) |

A "no" is information. Thank them, ask what would change their mind, and keep them on the update list only if they agree.

## 6. Meeting prep

- One-sentence description a stranger repeats; the 3-minute story (problem, wedge, proof, ask).
- The numbers you know cold: customers, revenue, growth, retention, burn, runway, CAC payback, cost to serve.
- The three questions that will hurt (a stronger competitor, concentration risk, why now) and honest answers.
- A demo that works offline or a recording as fallback.
- What you want from the meeting: a next meeting, an introduction, feedback, a term sheet. Ask for it.
- Questions for them: how they work with portfolio companies, how fast they decide, who else they involve, what would make them pass.
- Know the diligence list (data room list in the materials reference) and who in the team owns each item.

## 7. Tracker

Keep it in a sheet outside the repo (names and notes are personal data).

| Investor | Firm | Fit score | Route | Contacted | Replied | Meeting | Status | Next step | Date | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Status values: to contact, contacted, replied, meeting set, met, diligence, passed, in discussion, committed. Review weekly: how many contacted, replied, met; where the stall is (list quality, message, deck, proof).

## 8. Etiquette and law

- Do not send mass or blind-copied emails, do not scrape personal emails or phone numbers, honour any request to stop. Marketing and cold-email laws differ by country (see the consent notes in `lll:marketing`).
- Do not claim commitments you do not have ("our round is oversubscribed"). Do not name an investor as committed without their consent.
- Keep confidential information about customers, other investors and the term sheet confidential.
- Securities offering rules may limit how and to whom you can advertise an offering. Ask a lawyer before any public post about a raise.
- Be on time, be brief, answer what was asked.

## 9. Accelerators, grants and other money

For non-dilutive or early options, apply the same fit logic: eligibility, what they give, what they take, timelines, selection criteria. Record deadlines. Grants and programmes often want a one-pager and a short video; reuse the materials. Government schemes and recognition programmes change often: read the current official page.
