# Leads and outreach

For finding likely customers, qualifying them and writing outreach that gets replies. For investors use `lll:business`. Public information and the owner's own audience only: no scraped personal data, no bought lists.

## 1. Ideal customer profile (ICP)

Write it in one table before looking for a single lead.

| Field | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who (role or business type) | e.g. owner of a clinic with 1 to 3 doctors |
| Size and place | headcount, revenue band, city or region |
| Trigger events | opens a second branch, hires first employee, complains publicly, tax season, a rule change |
| Current workaround | paper, WhatsApp, spreadsheet, a rival tool |
| Budget holder and decision maker | same person or not |
| Disqualifiers | too small, wrong region, already locked into a contract |
| Where they gather | WhatsApp groups, trade associations, directories, events, LinkedIn, local markets |

## 2. Sources, best first

1. People who already raised a hand: replies, DMs, demo requests, waitlist, event attendees.
2. Warm introductions from customers and friends. Ask for them at the moment of success.
3. Communities and associations where the owner is a real participant.
4. Public directories and maps (trade directories, Google Maps listings, industry registers) for local businesses.
5. Public professional profiles and company sites for B2B roles.
6. Content that attracts inbound (posts, tools, calculators). `lll:seo` for search-led inbound.

Record the source of every lead. The source with the best reply and conversion rate gets more time.

## 3. Lead record and score

Minimum fields (a sheet or a CRM): name or business, role, contact route, source, date added, fit score, intent score, status, next action and date, notes. Do not store sensitive personal data you do not need. Keep the sheet out of the repo.

Score twice, each 0 to 3, then add:

| Fit (does the ICP match?) | Intent (are they in motion?) |
|---|---|
| 0 outside the ICP | 0 no sign of need |
| 1 partly matches | 1 follows or reads your content |
| 2 matches on type and size | 2 asked a question, visited pricing, replied |
| 3 matches and has a trigger | 3 asked for a demo or price |

5 to 6: contact today. 3 to 4: nurture with content. 0 to 2: leave.

## 4. Personalisation that is real

One sentence that shows you looked: a specific thing they posted, built, sold or said, or a trigger event. Never fake familiarity ("loved your recent post" without naming it). If you cannot find one real sentence, the lead is not ready for outreach.

## 5. Sequences

Four touches over 10 to 14 days, then stop. Use the channel where the person is active and where outreach is allowed (see section 8).

| Touch | Day | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | The observation, one clear value line, one easy question |
| 2 | 3 to 4 | A useful thing (a short demo, a checklist, a number), no pitch |
| 3 | 7 | A proof point from a similar customer, with permission |
| 4 | 12 to 14 | A short "closing the loop" note; offer to stop |

### Email template (under 120 words)

```
Subject: <specific, lowercase, about them or their problem>

Hi <first name>,

<one real observation about them>.

We help <their type> <outcome with number>. <Customer type> in <place> went from <before> to <after> in <time>.

Would a 10-minute look at how that works for <their situation> be useful? If not now, no problem.

<name>
<one line about who you are, with a real link>
```

### WhatsApp or DM template (under 50 words)

```
Hi <name>, <real observation>. I built <product> that helps <type> <outcome>. Happy to send a 1-minute video if useful?
```

### Reply handling

- Answer within hours. Reply to the question they asked first.
- "Not now": ask when to check back, and put the date in the sheet.
- "Not interested": thank them, stop, mark it.
- A price question: answer with the price, not another question.
- An objection: record it. Three of the same objection is a message or product problem, not a lead problem.

## 6. Tracker view

Weekly numbers: leads added, contacted, replied, calls or demos, trials, paid; by source. Reply rate under 5% on a real sample of 50: change the message or the segment before adding volume.

## 7. Cold outreach by the same owner at scale

Do not blast. Volume without relevance burns the sender domain and the owner's name. Send low volume from a warmed, authenticated domain (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), keep complaints near zero, and stop the sequence for anyone who replies, opts out or bounces.

## 8. Consent and rules (verify current text; not legal advice)

- **India:** the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and its rules govern personal data used for marketing; give notice, take consent where required, honour withdrawal. Commercial SMS and calls fall under the TRAI telemarketing rules (registered sender headers and templates, Do Not Disturb registry). WhatsApp business messaging needs the recipient's opt-in under the platform's policy.
- **EU and UK:** GDPR plus ePrivacy or PECR; cold email to individuals needs a lawful basis and consent in many cases; B2B rules differ by country.
- **US:** CAN-SPAM (opt-out, physical address, honest subject), TCPA for texts and calls.
- **Platforms:** LinkedIn, WhatsApp and Instagram ban automation and mass messaging; manual, relevant, low-volume outreach only.
- Keep a record of where each contact came from and when they agreed.

## 9. Never

- Buy or scrape lists of personal contacts.
- Pretend to be someone else, invent a mutual friend, or fake a referral.
- Promise results you cannot show.
- Keep messaging after a clear "stop".
- Put lead data in the repo, in a report, or in a third-party AI tool without the owner's approval.
