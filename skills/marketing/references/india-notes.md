# India notes (and what to change elsewhere)

Marketing defaults for an Indian audience. Treat them as hypotheses to test with the owner's own data, not facts about every buyer. Rules and platform terms change: verify the current text, and say these notes are not legal advice.

## 1. Audience patterns

- **Mobile first, Android first.** Many users are on mid-range or low-end Android phones, limited storage and patchy data. Short videos, small pages, no heavy downloads.
- **WhatsApp is the main app.** Customers expect to ask a question there and get a reply. A click-to-chat button and a fast human reply often outperform a sign-up form.
- **Trust is earned slowly and shown socially.** Local references, named customers (with permission), phone numbers that answer, a visible address and a real person on camera help. Reviews and "who else uses it" matter.
- **Price sensitivity is high and uneven.** Show the price early and include GST for consumers. Frame by day or by outcome ("less than a cup of chai a day") only if the maths is honest. A free first step lowers the barrier.
- **Language.** English reaches metros and professionals. Hindi and regional languages reach far wider. Test copy in the audience's language; for tier-2 and tier-3 audiences it is often the highest-impact change. Voice and subtitles in the spoken language.
- **Payments.** UPI is the default; offer UPI on every payment link. Cards are common among salaried urban users; cash on delivery still matters for physical goods. For subscriptions, UPI AutoPay needs an extra setup step; many users prefer a prepaid period. Pricing detail: `lll:pricing`.
- **Distribution partners.** Chartered accountants, distributors, shop-fitters, trade associations and local WhatsApp group admins are often the real channel for MSME products.
- **Creators.** Micro-creators (10k to 100k followers) in the audience's language and city often cost far less than large names and convert better for local products. Always agree a written scope and the disclosure label.

## 2. Festival and seasonal calendar

Dates move each year (lunar calendars). Check this year's dates and your region before planning.

| Period (approx.) | Moments | Typical angles |
|---|---|---|
| Jan | Makar Sankranti, Pongal, Lohri, Republic Day | New-year plans, harvest, patriotism (use carefully) |
| Feb to Mar | Holi, exam season, year-end for accounts and tax (31 March) | Study tools, tax filing, closing the books |
| Mar to Apr | New financial year, Ugadi, Gudi Padwa, Eid (varies), Baisakhi, Ram Navami | Fresh start, new books, new stock |
| Apr to Jun | Wedding season (varies by region), summer travel, school break | Events, travel, kids courses |
| Jul to Aug | Monsoon, Raksha Bandhan, Independence Day, Onam (Aug to Sep) | Gifting, home, sales |
| Sep to Oct | Ganesh Chaturthi, Navratri, Durga Puja, Dussehra | Big-spend season starts |
| Oct to Nov | Diwali (largest shopping peak), Dhanteras, Bhai Dooj | Gifting, stocking, offers, new accounts |
| Nov to Dec | Wedding season, Christmas, year-end | Gifting, year in review |
| Year round | IPL (cricket), board exams, appraisal season | Attention spikes, reference moments |

Start planning three weeks ahead. Deliver goodwill greetings from a person, not a template.

## 3. Rules to check before publishing (verify current text)

- **Advertising standards.** The Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) code and its guidelines on influencer advertising: disclose paid or gifted content upfront with a clear label (for example #ad or "Paid partnership"), no misleading claims, special rules for health, education and finance. The Consumer Protection Act 2019 and the consumer authority's guidelines on misleading advertisements and dark patterns also apply.
- **Finance and investing.** Promotion of investment products and advice is regulated (SEBI, RBI, IRDAI for insurance). Do not promise returns, do not give advice unless registered, and do not hire influencers to give unregistered advice. Ask a qualified person for sign-off.
- **Health and wellness.** No cure or treatment claims without proof and authority. Supplements, medical devices and clinics have extra rules.
- **Data protection.** The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and its rules: clear notice, consent for marketing use, a way to withdraw, security of stored contact data.
- **SMS and calls.** TRAI telemarketing rules: register as a sender, register message templates, respect the Do Not Disturb registry, send only in allowed hours.
- **WhatsApp.** Business messaging policy: opt-in, approved templates for outbound marketing, the 24-hour service window, pricing by message category.
- **Pricing display.** Consumer prices shown inclusive of tax; clear total before payment; auto-renewal terms visible.
- **Language and local sensitivities.** Maps, flags and religious imagery have specific rules and sensitivities; do not show disputed borders.

## 5. Outside India: what to change

| Item | Change |
|---|---|
| Rules | GDPR and ePrivacy in the EU and UK, CAN-SPAM and TCPA in the US, CASL in Canada, local advertising codes (FTC endorsement guides in the US, ASA in the UK) |
| Channels | WhatsApp is strong in Latin America, parts of Europe, Africa and the Middle East, weaker in the US where SMS and iMessage dominate; check by country |
| Payments | Cards and wallets by country (Apple Pay, Google Pay, Pix in Brazil, iDEAL in the Netherlands) |
| Pricing display | Tax exclusive in the US, inclusive in the EU and UK |
| Calendar | Local holidays and shopping peaks (Black Friday, Singles Day, Ramadan, Lunar New Year) |
| Language | Native reviewer for every language shipped |

Confirm each with a local source before relying on it.
