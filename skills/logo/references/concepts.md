# Concepts: generate, score, choose three

Ideas are cheap and builds are expensive. Write many ideas as sentences, choose three, and build only those.

## 1. Generate 8 to 12 one-sentence concepts

Format: `<mark type>: <the idea in one sentence>. Twist: <what makes it ownable>.`

Examples (fictional brand "Plinth", B2B invoicing):
- Lettermark: a capital P whose bowl is an open ledger line, read as both a letter and a rule on a page. Twist: the line extends past the bowl as the baseline of the wordmark.
- Abstract: a stacked block with a notch at the top right, a plinth that holds weight. Twist: the notch is a 45-degree cut that doubles as a corner fold.
- Pictorial: a simple column base. Twist: one side shorter, so it reads as a bar chart. (Test: too literal?)
- Wordmark: lowercase "plinth" with the i-dot replaced by a small square. Twist: the square becomes the favicon.

A concept that could describe a competitor's logo is not a concept. If it cannot be said in one sentence, it will not read in a glance.

## 2. Techniques that generate ideas

| Technique | How |
|---|---|
| Negative space | the shape is made by the gap between two others (an arrow in a letter, a figure in a counter) |
| Letter fusion | two letters share a stroke; a letter doubles as an object |
| Counterchange | figure and ground swap across a divider |
| Geometric reduction | start from the object and cut away until a few primitives remain |
| Rule-based form | the mark is built from a rule: concentric arcs, a quarter-circle grid, a single continuous line |
| Cut and fold | a diagonal cut, a notch, a folded corner creates a reading |
| Monoline | one stroke weight, rounded or squared ends, a continuous path |
| Modular grid | repeated units arranged to form the letter or shape |
| Motion cue | a direction or repeat implies movement (forward cut, rotation) |
| Paradox | a deliberate tension that is resolved on second look (heavy and light, open and closed) |

Mix techniques with the word map seeds. Prefer a seed from the name or the promise over an industry symbol.

## 3. Score quickly

Rate each concept 1 to 5 on five criteria:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Idea clarity | can I say it in one sentence, and would a stranger get it? |
| Distinction | does it stand apart from the category and from famous marks? |
| Simplicity | can it be drawn with a few shapes? |
| Relevance | does it match the adjectives and audience? |
| Small-size strength | does the silhouette survive 16 px? |

Add them up, then apply judgement. Pick the three strongest and most different (different mark types or at least different ideas), not three variations of one.

## 4. Library and originality check before building

- Search your memory and the web for the same subject plus technique. Use the browser or search tools read-only.
- Do a reverse image search on a quick render if tools allow.
- If your concept resembles a known mark, change it now. A trademark search by a professional is still needed later.

## 5. Build only three

- Draw each in black on a clean canvas (see the SVG construction notes). Do not polish ideas you will drop.
- Give each a short name ("Ledger P", "Plinth block", "Square dot").
- Test each at 16, 32, 64 and 256 px, in one colour and reversed, before deciding anything.

## 6. The checkpoint message

Show greyscale first (colour starts taste debates). Offer a small colour hint only for the recommended concept.

```
<image: the three concepts at large size, plus a row at 64 / 32 / 16 px>

A. <Name> (<mark type>)   <- recommended
   Idea: <one sentence>
   Why it fits: <2 or 3 bullets tied to the adjectives, audience, competitors>
   Risk: <one honest risk>

B. ...   C. ...

Recommendation: <one or two sentences>
Next: pick a direction, or tell me what you like in each. I can then build the full kit:
colour with one-colour and reversed versions, horizontal and stacked lockups, a small-size cut,
favicon and app-icon sets, a test sheet and a one-page usage guide.
```

Keep it short; the images carry the message. Do not attach variants, boards or icon sets yet. Then wait.

## 7. Handling feedback

- "I like A but want it friendlier": change one variable (terminal shape, corner radius, weight) and show before and after.
- "Can we combine A and B": test whether the combination keeps one idea; two ideas in one mark is a smell.
- "None": ask what is missing (feeling, shape, reference) and return with three new concepts from different seeds.
- A committee: present one recommendation with the criteria used; ask for feedback against the brief, not taste.
