# Colour and type for a logo

Colour and type come after the form works in black. They should be few, ownable, reproducible and accessible.

## 1. Colour

### Principles
- One or two colours. A third needs a reason. Colour supports the idea; it does not rescue it.
- Own something in the category: look at competitors' colours and avoid the shared default (fintech navy, wellness sage, startup purple gradient) unless you give it a fresh nuance.
- Meaning varies by culture: red (luck or danger), white (purity or mourning), green (nature, money, Islam), yellow (optimism, caution). Check the markets served.
- Greyscale must still separate: if two colours merge in greyscale, one-colour use will fail.

### Choosing
1. Start from the idea and the adjectives: calm, urgent, premium, friendly.
2. Pick a hue family from the subject's world (materials, places), not the category cliché.
3. Test the value: the mark must keep its form in black, white and in a lighter and a darker tone of itself.
4. Pair with a neutral: near-black and off-white that belong to the hue family.
5. Check contrast of the mark on the surfaces it will sit on (3:1 for graphical objects) and of the wordmark if it carries text meaning. For exact ratios, `lll:design-system` includes a contrast calculator.

### Reproduction
- Screen: HEX, RGB (sRGB), and OKLCH if the web stack uses it.
- Print: ask the printer for CMYK and a Pantone match; vivid screen blues, greens and oranges often fall outside CMYK gamut. Do not invent Pantone numbers; propose the nearest and mark "to be proofed".
- Provide: full colour, one-colour black, one-colour white (reversed), a single brand-colour version, and a greyscale check.
- Embroidery, engraving and vinyl: one colour, no hairlines, gaps at least about 1 mm at chest-logo size.

### Dark and light surfaces
A colour logo often needs two versions: one for light backgrounds and one for dark. Do not just invert; re-pick tints so contrast and brand colour hold. Keep a white-only version for photos and busy backgrounds.

## 2. Type

### Wordmark type study
1. Name the personality: geometric (precise, modern), humanist (friendly), grotesk (neutral, honest), serif (heritage, editorial), slab (sturdy), script (personal; risky small), mono (technical).
2. Shortlist three faces with different characters; set the name in each at large size and at 32 px high.
3. Customise: at least one or two letters (a cut, a joined pair, a unique terminal, a custom dot) so the wordmark is ownable. Draw the changes as outlines.
4. Fix spacing optically (see the refinement notes) and check weight against the symbol.
5. Choose case: lowercase feels friendly, all caps formal and wide, title case neutral. Consider how it breaks across two lines for stacked lockups.

### Pairing with the symbol
- Match stroke weight: the wordmark's stem width relates to the symbol's stroke (same, or a simple ratio).
- Match geometry: round symbol with round-leaning letters; angular symbol with straighter letters.
- Align on a shared baseline or cap height, and fix the gap between symbol and wordmark as a multiple of a unit (for example the x-height).
- The wordmark must work alone; the symbol should work alone.

### Licences
- Use fonts whose licence allows logo use and outlining. Open licences (SIL OFL, Apache) generally allow it; commercial licences vary; system fonts may not.
- State the family, foundry and licence in the guide. If unsure, tell the owner to check with the foundry. Do not decide legal questions.
- Because the final is outlined, the font does not ship with the logo, but the right to create the artwork matters.

### Taglines
Keep the tagline out of the primary mark. Provide a lockup with tagline and a version without, and set a minimum size for the tagline version (taglines die below about 120 px wide).

## 3. Lockups

Define these with fixed proportions:
- **Horizontal** (symbol left, wordmark right): headers, documents.
- **Stacked** (symbol above wordmark): square spaces, social, signage.
- **Symbol only:** app icon, favicon, avatar, stamp.
- **Wordmark only:** where the symbol already appears nearby.
- **Small-size cut** where needed.

For each: the relative size of symbol and wordmark, the gap, alignment, and clear space (usually the height of a defined element of the mark, such as the dot, on all sides).

## 4. Checks

1. Works in black, white and the brand colour on light and dark surfaces.
2. Greyscale keeps separation.
3. Colour count is 1 or 2 (3 with a reason).
4. CMYK and Pantone to be proofed, noted.
5. Wordmark has at least one customised detail and fixed optical spacing.
6. Font licences stated; text outlined.
7. Lockups defined with proportions and clear space.
