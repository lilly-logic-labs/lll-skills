# Mark types and how to choose

Explore at least two different types before picking. The type is a strategic decision, not a style.

## 1. The types

| Type | What it is | Strengths | Risks | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Wordmark (logotype)** | The name set or drawn as the logo | Name recognition; unambiguous | Weak at 16 px; needs a compact companion for icons | distinctive or short names; brands that rely on name |
| **Lettermark** | Initials or one letter | Compact, scalable | Generic if the letterform is stock | long names; professional services; apps |
| **Monogram** | Interlocked or ornamented initials | Personal, crafted | Detail can clog; legibility | studios, fashion, hospitality |
| **Pictorial mark** | A recognisable object | Memorable, quick | Literalness; clichés; hard to own | simple, concrete offerings |
| **Abstract mark** | A non-representational form | Ownable, flexible, meaning added by use | Needs investment to mean something | broad, technical or new categories |
| **Emblem / badge** | Name inside a crest, seal or container | Heritage, craft, authority | Fine detail dies at small size | cafés, breweries, schools, institutions |
| **Combination** | Symbol plus wordmark in lockups | Most flexible; symbol grows into the identity | Must work apart and together | most new brands |
| **Mascot / character** | A drawn character | Warm, shareable | Costly to keep consistent; childish if wrong audience | consumer, kids, community |
| **Dynamic / system** | A core with variable parts | Many touchpoints, many products | Needs strict rules; poor for tiny brands | platforms, events, large organisations |
| **Letterform with idea** | A letter with a meaningful cut or counter | Ownable, compact | The hidden idea must read | brands whose initial suggests an idea |

## 2. Decision guide

Ask in order:
1. **Where is the smallest, first use?** App icon or favicon: you need a compact symbol or letter. Header only: a wordmark may do.
2. **How distinctive is the name?** A rare, short name can carry a wordmark. A common or long name needs a symbol or a letterform.
3. **How concrete is the offering?** Concrete and simple: a pictorial idea might work. Abstract or technical: a geometric or abstract mark.
4. **What is the personality?** Heritage and craft lean emblem or serif wordmark. Modern and technical lean geometric symbols and sans wordmarks.
5. **How many touchpoints?** Many products or sub-brands: a dynamic system around a rigid core.
6. **Who is the audience?** Children and community brands tolerate characters; regulated or financial brands usually need restraint.

Default for most new brands: a combination of a simple symbol (or a customised initial) and a clean wordmark, plus a compact version for icons.

## 3. Craft by type

- **Wordmark:** choose or modify a face with intent. Customise at least one or two letters (a cut, a joined pair, a unique terminal) so it is ownable. Fix spacing optically, not with default kerning. Check all letter pairs, the capital I vs lowercase l, and rn vs m.
- **Lettermark / monogram:** the letter must stay legible first. Test with the "what letter is this?" question.
- **Pictorial:** reduce until only the idea remains; find the angle nobody uses; avoid literal depictions of the product (a tooth for a dentist).
- **Abstract:** derive it from a rule (a grid, a motion, a relationship, a letter of the name); a rule gives the mark a reason to exist and a system to grow.
- **Emblem:** design the simple version first (container plus one central idea) and treat ornament as optional; make a no-container version for small sizes.
- **Combination:** design the lockups together: shared radii, stroke logic and weights; define horizontal, stacked and symbol-only versions with fixed proportions.

## 4. Meaning without literalness

The strongest marks evoke the attitude or promise instead of drawing the product. Sources of meaning: the name's sound or spelling, the founding story, a verb the product performs (connect, fold, release), a structural relationship (two shapes making a third), or a place or material. Write the idea as one sentence; if it needs a paragraph, it will not read in a glance.

## 5. Things to avoid

- Stock typefaces unmodified as the whole idea.
- Clip-art depictions of the industry.
- Over-used devices: swoosh, globe, lightbulb, handshake, sparkle, generic hexagon network, leaf with a circuit.
- Two ideas competing in one mark.
- Hidden meaning that nobody can see; make it a bonus, not the point.
- Details smaller than about 1/48 of the mark's height.
- Resemblance to a famous mark; when in doubt, change it.
