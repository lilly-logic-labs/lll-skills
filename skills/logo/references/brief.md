# Logo brief: discovery, category scan, word map

A logo is judged against its purpose, so write the purpose down first. Keep the process light: short questions, written assumptions, quick concepts.

## 1. Read before asking

Search the repository for: existing logos and icons (`*.svg`, `public/`, `assets/`, `brand/`, app icon sets, `manifest.json`), `DESIGN.md`, README, landing copy, pitch or business docs, app store text. Take the exact spelling and capitalisation of the name from there. Ask only what is still missing.

## 2. Questions (ask at most five in one message)

1. The name, exactly as it should appear (capitalisation, spacing, symbols such as "&"), and any tagline that must lock up with it.
2. What the business does and for whom, in one sentence a customer would use.
3. Three to five adjectives for how it should feel, and two it must never feel.
4. Two or three competitors or admired brands, and one look the owner dislikes.
5. Where the mark must work first (app icon, website header, packaging, signage, merch, print) and any fixed constraints (existing colours, equity to keep, platforms, languages and scripts).

If the owner wants speed, skip the questions: write the assumptions as a short list and go to concepts. A concrete concept draws better feedback than a questionnaire.

## 3. The brief (write it, keep it short)

```
Name: <exact>      Tagline: <if any>
Offering: <one sentence>
Audience: <who, where they meet the mark>
Adjectives: <3 to 5>     Never: <2 to 3>
Competitors / peers: <list>   Dislikes: <look>
Must work: <contexts, smallest size, one-colour needs>
Constraints: <colours, equity, scripts, legal marks>
Decision maker: <who signs off>
Assumptions: <marked, to confirm>
```

## 4. Adjectives become visual cues

| Adjective | Try |
|---|---|
| Trustworthy, stable | symmetry, solid weights, closed forms, steady baseline |
| Precise, technical | geometric construction, exact angles, monoline, small counters |
| Friendly, warm | rounded terminals, open counters, soft curves, slight asymmetry |
| Premium, quiet | high contrast strokes, generous spacing, restraint, a single detail |
| Fast, energetic | forward slant, open shapes, diagonal cuts, tight spacing |
| Playful | irregular rhythm, bold fill, a smile or surprise in a counter |
| Natural, handmade | organic curves, uneven weights, texture only if it survives reproduction |
| Heritage, craft | emblem or seal structure, serif letterforms, ornament kept simple |

Anti-adjectives remove cues just as strongly ("never corporate" rules out the blue swoosh).

## 5. Word map

Write the name, the offering, the adjectives and the promise on one page. Around them list nouns, verbs, metaphors, materials, objects, places, opposites, and sounds. Circle intersections between the offering and the adjectives. Each strong intersection is a concept seed. Prefer seeds derived from the name or the promise over generic symbols of the industry.

Example: a bakery called "Harbor & Pine": harbour (anchor, mast, tide line, horizon), pine (needle, cone, triangle), bakery (crust, wheat, oven arch). Intersection: a mast and a wheat stalk share the same vertical stroke; a pine triangle sits under a tide line.

## 6. Category scan

1. Name the category conventions: colours, symbols, type style (fintech: navy, shield, upward arrow; coffee: bean, cup, steam; AI: spark, node, gradient orb; legal: scales, serif; fitness: figure, bolt).
2. Look at 8 to 12 competitor marks (browser tools, read-only). Write what they share.
3. Decide what to share on purpose (people must recognise the category) and where to depart (people must remember you).
4. Cliché list: anything on the shared list is off limits unless you give it a genuinely fresh form.

## 7. Equity and redesign constraints

If a mark already exists: what do people recognise (a colour, a shape, a name style)? Keep that, change the rest. Document what must stay. Refresh vs rebrand decision is in the critique and redesign notes.

## 8. Naming problems you may meet

- Very long names: wordmark in two lines or an initial-based symbol.
- Names with common letter pairs (rn vs m, I vs l): choose a type or customisation that separates them.
- Names that are hard to read at small sizes (many ascenders and descenders): a compact lettermark for tiny uses.
- Non-Latin scripts: design the mark in each script or make the symbol carry the identity; check licences of fonts for that script.
- Names that spell something unintended when abbreviated: check initials.

## 9. Checks before concepts

1. Name spelling confirmed.
2. Adjectives and anti-adjectives recorded.
3. Smallest and first context known.
4. Cliché list written.
5. Assumptions labelled.
